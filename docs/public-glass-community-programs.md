# Public Glass Community Program Documentation

The public sees the glow.

The team sees the signups, waivers, bench flow, PPE, volunteer schedule, instructor staffing, pickup plan, thank-you notes, budget, cleanup, and the person who is definitely going to stand in the wrong place unless someone tells them kindly and early.

That invisible work is the program. The glow is the part people remember.

This file is a structure for documenting community-facing glass programs. It is not official Public Glass policy. It is a way to keep the learning from disappearing after the event.

## Purpose

Before designing the glass activity, answer the human question.

```text
Who is this for?
Why are they here?
What should they feel, learn, make, or understand?
What would make the program successful?
What would make it unsafe, inaccessible, or not worth repeating?
```

Glass is seductive. It is very easy to design around the object and forget the people. Do not do that.

## Common formats

### Intro class

Good for first contact with the medium.

Document:

- Object made
- Required staff-to-participant ratio
- Time per participant
- What participants can safely do
- What instructors must do
- Pickup process
- Common questions

### Demonstration

Good for awe, education, fundraising, or public visibility.

Document:

- Demo object
- Narration plan
- Sight lines
- Heat perimeter
- Microphone or sound needs
- Crowd flow
- How questions are handled

### Community group session

Good for access, belonging, and a shared experience with a specific group.

Document:

- Partner organization
- Participant needs
- Transportation constraints
- Cost coverage
- Accessibility considerations
- Translation needs
- Follow-up plan

### Volunteer event

Good for studio support and community building.

Document:

- Roles
- Shift lengths
- Training required
- Food and water plan
- Safety briefing
- Cleanup responsibilities
- Thank-you plan

## Story capture

Capture more than the finished object.

- Arrival and welcome
- Safety briefing
- Hands, tools, and process
- Instructor explanation
- Teamwork moments
- Safe-distance crowd reactions
- Finished objects
- Participant quotes, with permission
- The reason the program mattered beyond "we made glass"

## Post-program debrief

Do this while people still remember what actually happened.

```text
What worked?
What was confusing?
What took longer than expected?
What safety issues or near misses occurred?
What access needs were missed or handled well?
What should change next time?
What photos, quotes, or outcomes can be shared?
Who needs a thank-you?
Who needs follow-up?
Where are the finished pieces now?
```

## Metrics that matter

| Metric | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Participants served | Scale and reach. |
| First-time glass participants | Access and pipeline. |
| Partner organizations | Community relationships. |
| Teaching artists paid | Sustainability for artists. |
| Volunteer hours | True program cost and community investment. |
| Scholarships or subsidized spots | Access. |
| Repeat participation | Whether the program creates a path, not just a moment. |
| Participant quotes | Human value that numbers miss. |
| Safety incidents / near misses | How the next program gets safer. |

## Public Glass context

Public Glass describes itself as San Francisco's center for glass, art, and education. Its public site points people toward classes, events, memberships, rentals, hot shop, flame shop, kiln/fusing, stained glass, cold shop, team events, Light a Spark community groups, Hot Glass Cold Beer, and volunteer opportunities.

That range matters. It means a program is not just a standalone activity. It can be a doorway into a larger glass community, if someone bothers to make the doorway visible.
