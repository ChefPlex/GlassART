# GlassART

**Notes, resources, and tools at the intersection of glassblowing and everything else.**

I've been blowing glass for years - one or two nights a week, usually at Public Glass in San Francisco's Bayview neighborhood, where I also serve as Board President. Glass is not a hobby I picked up to relax. It's the thing that taught me how to work with heat, timing, and irreversibility in ways that no amount of program management ever quite did. You get one gather. The physics are not negotiable. There are no rollbacks.

This repo is where I collect things that live at the edge of that practice - resources for other artists, notes on technique, tools I've built, and the occasional project that started in the studio and ended up somewhere more public. My work is at [artglasssf.com](https://www.artglasssf.com).

---

## About the Practice

Glassblowing is a team sport that looks like a solo act. The gaffer at the pipe is visible; the assistant gathering the next color, managing the heat, handing off the tools at exactly the right moment - less so. I've been both. The collaboration is most of it.

The disciplines I work in:

- **Hot glass / glassblowing** - The furnace work. Gathering, shaping, inflating, finishing. Vases, bowls, paperweights, cocktail glass. The version most people picture when they hear the word.
- **Flameworking** - Borosilicate work at the torch. More controlled, more intimate, completely different set of instincts.
- **Cold work** - Cutting, grinding, polishing. What you do after the heat is done and the piece is out of the kiln.

The studio is [Public Glass](https://www.publicglass.org), 1750 Armstrong Avenue, San Francisco. Nonprofit, open access, Bayview/Hunters Point. If you're in the Bay Area and want to try glassblowing, it's the right place to start.

### Artist Budget & Pricing Tool

**File:** `Artist_Budget_Pricing_Tool.xlsx`

The companion spreadsheet to the GAS presentation. Fill in the yellow cells; everything else calculates automatically.

Six sections:

| Section | What It Does |
|---|---|
| Budget & Survival Costs | Your share of monthly living costs - housing, food, health, transportation, all of it. Shared-household aware. |
| Other Income Offsets | Partner income, pensions, part-time work - what reduces what your art actually needs to earn. |
| Annual Living Cost (net) | Strips out other income so you see the real gap your practice needs to close. |
| Annual Business Costs | Studio access, materials, marketing, shows, shipping, equipment - the full cost of running a glass practice. |
| Taxes & Savings | The one nobody warns you about. Self-employment tax is ~15.3% before income tax. 25-30% set aside is not optional. |
| Revenue Goal | The number. Not what you'd like to make. What you need to make just to be okay. |

A second sheet, **Pricing Calculator**, walks through:

- Your required hourly rate, reverse-engineered from your revenue goal
- Skilled trade rate benchmarks (electricians, plumbers, carpenters - people who also work with their hands after years of training)
- Gallery consignment math with your effective hourly rate after the split
- Cost-plus pricing for a single piece, floor price first
- A blended income model so you can see which streams close the gap

A **Quick Reference** sheet has the formulas, real-talk answers to the questions that always come up (LLC vs. separate bank account, how to say no to unpaid work, what to do when a commission goes sideways), and the one-liner on imposter syndrome that tends to land.
