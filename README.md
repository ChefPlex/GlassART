# GlassART

**Notes, resources, and tools at the intersection of glassblowing and everything else.**

I've been blowing glass for years - one or two nights a week, usually at Public Glass in San Francisco's Bayview neighborhood, where I also served as Board President. Glass is not a hobby I picked up to relax. It is the thing that taught me how to work with heat, timing, and irreversibility in ways that no amount of program management ever quite did. You get one gather. The physics are not negotiable. There are no rollbacks.

This repo is where I collect things that live at the edge of that practice - resources for other artists, notes on technique, tools I've built, and the occasional project that started in the studio and ended up somewhere more public. My work is at [artglasssf.com](https://www.artglasssf.com), which is the more finished side of the house. This repo is the shop table. Useful things, half-burned notes, tools that work, tools that need another pass, and the occasional reminder that just because you can put glass in a kiln does not mean you should put that glass in that kiln.

## About the Practice

Glassblowing is a team sport that looks like a solo act. The gaffer at the pipe is visible; the assistant gathering the next color, managing the heat, handing off the tools at exactly the right moment - less so. I've been both. The collaboration is most of it.

The disciplines I work in:

- **Hot glass / glassblowing** - The furnace work. Gathering, shaping, inflating, finishing. Vases, bowls, paperweights, cocktail glass. The version most people picture when they hear the word.
- **Flameworking** - Torch work, also called lampworking or torchworking: solid rods or hollow tubes heated in a stationary flame, then shaped, sculpted, or blown with tools, gravity, and breath. Borosilicate lives here, but so does soft glass, bead work, marbles, pendants, small sculpture, and tiny hollow forms. More controlled, more intimate, completely different set of instincts.
- **Cold work** - Cutting, grinding, polishing. What you do after the heat is done and the piece is out of the kiln, when the piece stops glowing and starts telling you what you actually made.

The studio is [Public Glass](https://www.publicglass.org), 1750 Armstrong Avenue, San Francisco. Nonprofit, open access, Bayview/Hunters Point. If you're in the Bay Area and want to try glassblowing, it is the right place to start.

## What's In This Repo

### The Business of Being an Artist - GAS 2026 Presentation

**Files:** `Business_Of_Being_An_Artist_Presentation.html`, `index.html`

A full slide presentation built for the Glass Art Society 2026 conference. The subject is the one most glass artists avoid until it becomes a crisis: pricing, income, and the business of sustaining a practice.

The presentation covers:

- Why most glass artists are working for $8-12 an hour - and why that is a math problem, not an art problem
- How to reverse-engineer a real revenue goal from your actual living costs
- The gallery consignment calculation most artists have never run (run it before you sign anything)
- Why underpricing is almost never about numbers - it is about fear, and naming that is half the fix
- One platform done well versus five done badly
- Having a day job as a strategy, not a failure (Philip Glass drove a cab at 41, after Einstein on the Beach)
- Multiple income streams as a resilient practice, not a patchwork compromise
- The business basics that can wait, and the ones that cannot

The deck was built to be used live, with presenter notes throughout and a companion worksheet handed out at minute 15, not minute 55. Both files are here.

### Artist Budget & Pricing Tool

**File:** `Artist_Budget_Pricing_Tool.xlsx`

The companion spreadsheet to the GAS presentation. Fill in the yellow cells; everything else calculates automatically.

Six sections:

| Section | What It Does |
|---|---|
| Budget & Survival Costs | Your share of monthly living costs - housing, food, health, transportation, all of it. Shared-household aware. |
| Other Income Offsets | Partner income, pensions, part-time work - what reduces what your art actually needs to earn. |
| Annual Living Cost (net) | Strips out other income so you see the real gap your practice needs to close. |
| Annual Business Costs | Studio access, materials, marketing, shows, shipping, equipment - the full cost of running a glass practice. |
| Taxes & Savings | The one nobody warns you about. Self-employment tax is roughly 15.3% before income tax. 25-30% set aside is not optional. |
| Revenue Goal | The number. Not what you'd like to make. What you need to make just to be okay. |

A second sheet, **Pricing Calculator**, walks through:

- Your required hourly rate, reverse-engineered from your revenue goal
- Skilled trade rate benchmarks (electricians, plumbers, carpenters - people who also work with their hands after years of training)
- Gallery consignment math with your effective hourly rate after the split
- Cost-plus pricing for a single piece, floor price first
- A blended income model so you can see which streams close the gap

A **Quick Reference** sheet has the formulas, real-talk answers to the questions that always come up (LLC vs. separate bank account, how to say no to unpaid work, what to do when a commission goes sideways), and the one-liner on imposter syndrome that tends to land.
